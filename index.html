Research

Trade Transparency and Buyer Learning

This paper studies trade transparency in a market where a long-lived informed seller repeatedly meets short-lived buyers who can acquire costly private information before making offers. Transparency allows later buyers to observe whether earlier trade occurred, but not transaction prices. When there are no gains from trade with the low-quality seller, transparency can induce inefficient overtrading; when such gains exist, it can instead generate strategic delay. Transparency may reduce buyers’ incentives to acquire private information compared to an opaque market. Whether transparency raises or lowers welfare depends on prior beliefs, signal precision, and seller patience.
